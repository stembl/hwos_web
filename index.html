About the book

The gap between the decision and the test result

Hardware programs fail in a predictable pattern: requirements drift from what the physics actually demands, decisions get made without a clear owner, and the test results arrive too late to change what was already built. Everyone is working. No one agrees on what "locked" means.

The Hardware OS is the operating procedure for hardware programs — a shared rule for how requirements are written, how decisions get made and recorded, how evidence changes the plan, and how that discipline is installed in an organization that is already in motion.

This is not a management theory book. It is a practitioner's guide written by an engineer who has run programs that failed the old way and then fixed them.

Who this is for

Written for engineers running programs, not projects

  • Engineering leads who are responsible for a hardware program from concept to production and find that the build always diverges from the decisions made in the review room.
  • Systems engineers who write requirements and watch them get softened, stacked with margin, or quietly abandoned as schedules compress.
  • Program managers on hardware programs who need a technically credible framework — not a generic PM methodology — for keeping the program honest as evidence arrives.

About the author

Steve Embleton

Steve Embleton is a hardware engineering lead with experience running power electronics programs from concept through production. He has worked on programs where the OS described in this book was missing — and on programs where it was installed partway through, under schedule pressure, and worked.

He also writes simulation training for power electronics engineers at simulation.thehardwareos.com.
